## Goal
`apps/api/src/kernel/events/ordered-event-worker.test.ts` › "runs a replayed event again with fresh attempts, and the stream continues behind it" failed on main CI 40066380468 with "timed out waiting for both events to be handled in order". After this card the test passes the same way on a slow runner.

## Inputs read
- the test (around line 308) and `waitFor` in `apps/api/src/test-support/wait-for.ts` (15 s default)
- `kernel/events/event-worker.ts`: a job ahead of its stream waits with `moveToDelayed`, starting at `STREAM_WAIT_BASE_DELAY_MS` (1 s) and doubling up to `STREAM_WAIT_MAX_DELAY_MS` (60 s) (ADR-115)
- `05-CONVENTIONS.md` "Testing" (3.26)

## Cause
The second event waits behind the dead-lettered first one. While the first event uses up its attempts, the second keeps waiting, and each wait doubles. After about four waits the next wait is more than 15 s. So when the replay fixes the first event, the second may sleep longer than the test waits. Production is correct: ADR-115 caps a follower's delay at 60 s by design. The test assumes a wake-up time that the design does not promise.

## Decisions
1. **Fix the test, not the worker.** After `replayDeadLetter` and after the first event is handled again, the test promotes the second event's delayed job (`job.promote()` on the job from `buildEventJobId(second.eventId, HANDLER_KEY)`, when its state is `delayed`). The scenario stays the same: the second event was waiting behind the dead letter, and the test still asserts the order `[1, 2]`, `REPLAYED`, fresh attempts and `requeuedAt`.
2. Add a comment that says why: the follower's wake-up is bounded by the backoff cap (ADR-115), not by the replay.
3. Look for the same pattern in `ordered-stream-follow.test.ts` and `dead-letter-replayer.test.ts`: a `waitFor` on a follower that may be in a long `delayed` wait. Fix each one the same way, or list them in the handoff with the reason they are safe.
4. No change to production code, `waitFor` or the constants.
